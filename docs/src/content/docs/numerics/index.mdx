simgeometry is the geometry of the simulation codes, nothing physical: shapes with an inside
test, a signed distance and a normal; the first crossing of a particle's step with them; the
cut-cell data of a grid; and the uniform grids and the MPI process grid that splits them.
Every array query takes component-major points $p$ of shape `(ndim, n)` and runs on NumPy or,
through cunumpy, on CuPy; the hot loops are compiled Pyccel kernels.

Each equation on these pages is declared on the code that implements it, as a method card, and
checked by the tests: 19 closed forms (the level sets of the shapes, the Boolean and affine
level sets, the analytic crossings) against SymPy rules, three discretisations against their
order of convergence. The pages of the section list the cards module by module; this one shows
how they fit together.

## Shapes

A shape is a set $S$ with a membership test (`contains_points`) and, where it has an analytic
description, a level set $\phi$: negative inside, zero on the boundary, the exact Euclidean
signed distance where `level_set_exact` is true and a bound on it otherwise. An inverted shape is
the complement, with the sign of $\phi$ flipped
([shape conventions](/simgeometry/numerics/shapes/)). The level sets are the standard exact
distances of boxes, segments, capped cones and tori:

- [Line](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-line/), [Circle](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-circle/),
  [Sphere](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-sphere/);
- boxes with rounded edges: [Rectangle](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-rectangle/),
  [Cuboid](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-cuboid/);
- polygons by the even-odd rule: [Polygon](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-polygon/),
  [Triangle](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-triangle/),
  [Aperture2D](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-aperture2d/), and the profile of
  [Aperture3D](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-aperture3d/) (no level set);
- [solids of revolution](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-axisymmetric/), whose distance is that
  of their profile in the half-plane $(s, \rho)$:
  [Cylinder](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-cylinder/),
  [Tube](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-tube/), [Cone](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-cone/),
  [Torus](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-torus/), and the
  [SlantedTube](/simgeometry/numerics/objects-slanted-tube/), a bound near its cut;
- [Booleans, affine transforms and sections](/simgeometry/numerics/operations/), whose level
  sets are bounds, and [collections](/simgeometry/numerics/collection/) of labelled objects.

2D shapes also give their boundary as counter-clockwise loops (`outlines`, second order in the
spacing for curved boundaries) and as [boundary panels](/simgeometry/numerics/panels/) for
boundary-element solvers.

## Crossings

A particle pusher tests the whole step $a \to b$ of every marker, not only its end, so a fast
particle cannot pass through a thin plate. [First crossings](/simgeometry/numerics/crossings/)
are analytic for balls, boxes, polygons, slanted tubes and their affine images; other shapes
are sampled along the step and refined by Illinois steps on the level set, or bisected on
membership without one.

<MethodCard name="simgeometry.crossings.crossings" headingLevel={3} />

The compiled [swept absorption](/simgeometry/numerics/polygon-objects/) of 2D markers runs the
same [segment-edge test](/simgeometry/numerics/kernels-polygon-hits/) against packed polygon
objects in one pass and returns the object each marker hit first.

## Cut cells

[Edge crossings](/simgeometry/numerics/queries/) locate where the edges of a grid enter a shape
(Illinois on the level set, bisection otherwise); [cut edges](/simgeometry/numerics/cut-edges/)
collect them per grid edge for embedded-boundary finite differences.
[Cut-cell fractions](/simgeometry/numerics/fractions/) give the inside part of every cell by
midpoint quadrature, and of every edge and face of a Yee grid, with the trace of the surface,
for conformal field solvers.

## Grids and decomposition

A [grid](/simgeometry/numerics/grid/) has uniform nodes per axis, the first and last on the
bounds (one spacing short of the upper bound on a periodic axis). The
[domain decomposition](/simgeometry/numerics/domain-decomposition/) splits it over a process
grid with row-major ranks: one near-even split of the nodes, shared by the field arrays
(mpiarray) and the markers, whose owner follows from the subdomain edges. The
[particle boundaries](/simgeometry/numerics/simulation-domain/) of the box kill, reflect, wrap
or reinject markers per axis.

## Conventions

Lengths are in the units of the caller. Shapes and grids are axis-aligned unless an axis or a
matrix is given. Normals have unit length and point out of the region where `contains` is
true, so they flip for inverted shapes. Fractions along a step lie in $(0, 1]$: a crossing at
the very start ($t \le 10^{-12}$) is not reported, one exactly at the end is. Boundary
conventions of the inside tests differ: `Cuboid`, `Line` and the balls are closed; `Rectangle`,
`Polygon`, `Triangle` and `Aperture3D` use the half-open bounding box $l \le p < u$.

## Not covered

- No winding-number test: self-intersecting polygons follow the even-odd rule.
- The absorption kernel reports the object only, not the hit point, edge or normal, and tests
  the end points of long (wrapped) steps only.
- Sampled crossings miss features thinner than a step over `samples`; Boolean, section and
  collection level sets are bounds, so `signed_distance` falls back to a sampled mesh for them.
- `Aperture3D` and function objects have no level set and no analytic crossing; a section of a
  3D shape only bounds the in-plane distance.
- Halo exchange, ghost cells and field layouts are mpiarray's; simgeometry hands over the
  process grid and the halo width.
- The process-grid search does not balance the axes and never fails.
- The cut-cell fractions have no single order: the Yee fractions are exact for planes and second
  order or better for curved surfaces, the midpoint fractions are multiples of $n^{-d}$.

## Verification

Besides the checks of the cards, the tests compare every vectorised inside test with the scalar
one, the compiled kernels (Pyccel, NumPy, CUDA in emulation) with each other and with the
shapes, the level sets with exact distances and the Boolean bounds, the analytic crossings with
dense sampling, the Illinois roots with bisection (fewer than 10 level-set evaluations per edge
against 50 membership tests), the Yee fractions with planes (exact) and with a circle and a
sphere (area, perimeter and volume), and the decomposition with brute-force owners and with
runs on 4 and 6 MPI ranks.
